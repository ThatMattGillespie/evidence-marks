Sources cited, Source and Full entry as one system

The pane has three levels. A chip lands on whichever level fits the source, so the way around has to work from either landing place, and above all from the Full entry, where most full-text citations open, deep in the paper.

Full entryThe paper, scrolled to the cited section
  • A full text chip lands on the Full entry, at its highlighted section (Source is skipped).
  • An abstract-only or web chip, or a card in the list, lands on the Source.
  • Today the arrow in each head goes up one level without saying where: from the Full entry, two clicks to reach the list.

Reading the three

  • 1 · Breadcrumb is the most literal answer: every level one click away from any other, and it says where you are. The Full entry's wide pane has room for all three steps.
  • 2 · Up, and All keeps today's arrow (one level up, now named) and adds one fixed "All 6 sources" link in the same spot in every view. Quieter, and the shortest change to explain.
  • 3 · Step through adds a way across: the next cited source opens where its chip would (a full text at its passage). It suits a reader checking claim after claim, which is your question for Ahn.
  • In every version, the Full entry's head stays in view while the paper scrolls, carrying the chip and short title, so a reader who lands mid-paper knows which source this is. On a phone that bar exists already; this gives it to the desk too.
  • 1 or 2 can ship in this PR. 3 is a behaviour change and fits a follow-up, after Ahn's answer.
