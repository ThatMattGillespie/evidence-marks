What does the evidence say about reintegrating former combatants in Afghanistan, and what has worked elsewhere?

Peace Intel

Reintegration programmes in Afghanistan stalled wherever security guarantees for former combatants were weak 11. Fighters who disarmed without protection were often targeted, and many returned to armed groups within two years.

Community-based reintegration held up better than cash-only packages 22, and cash transfers alone are rated as having mixed effects across studies 33.

What has worked elsewhere

Sierra Leone's programme is the clearest success on record 44, with re-recruitment under 8% in districts that paired payments with local mediation 55.

The picture in Afghanistan may be changing. Recent field reporting suggests militias in Kunduz re-recruited many fighters 66, a pattern also described in an unpublished review 77. Both are worth checking before relying on them.
