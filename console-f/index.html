What does the evidence say about reintegrating former combatants in Afghanistan, and what has worked elsewhere?

Peace Intel

Reintegration programmes in Afghanistan stalled wherever security guarantees for former combatants were weak1. Fighters who disarmed without protection were often targeted, and many returned to armed groups within two years.

Community-based reintegration held up better than cash-only packages2, and cash transfers alone are rated as having mixed effects across studies32.

What has worked elsewhere

Sierra Leone's programme is the clearest success on record4, with re-recruitment under 8% in districts that paired payments with local mediation5.

The picture in Afghanistan may be changing. Recent field reporting suggests militias in Kunduz re-recruited many fighters6, which is worth checking against the library before relying on it.
